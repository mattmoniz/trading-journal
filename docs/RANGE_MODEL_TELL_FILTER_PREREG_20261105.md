# Pre-registration: does the next-hour range forecast sort the tell's explore trades? (a simulated approve/veto view; written 2026-11-04 night BEFORE any tell dollar outcome was joined to a forecast)

STATUS: FROZEN. Origin: user, 2026-11-04 ('Preregister it'), after asking for a P&L chart 'against all trades with a historical toggle like the original ML' for the range model, which has no trades of its own (`docs/STRUCTURE_SHADOW_MODEL_DESIGN_20261105.md`). Research only: nothing live, no order, no gate, no size change. Ledger family `range_model_tell_filter_20261105`, 3 counted tests. This is the SIMULATED analogue of the meta-labeler's All / Approved / Vetoed chart; it is not a trading rule.

## 1. The question, in plain words
When the model expects a WIDE next hour, does the tell's trade entered in that hour do better than when it expects a NARROW hour? If yes, the model could one day veto tell trades; if no, the model stays a forecast display and the dollar chart is not worth building.

## 2. Population and data (all files exist; none is re-derived)
- Trades: the 1,760 explore tell entries of `scripts/amt_vbars/trades_V1_explore.csv`, 2023-04-04 to 2025-05-16, replayed by the live shadow-book code (`scripts/tell_context/research_20261103/tell_replay_lib.py`, hr-022); rows in `scratch/tell_context_20261103/tell_stop_placement_rows.csv`. PRIMARY outcome = book A net dollars (the tell as it runs; parity with `csv_net` already checked). Books B (edge stop) and C (flip) are reported, not counted.
- Forecast: the walk-forward predictions of `scratch/structure_model_20261105/rows_walkforward.csv` (`p2` = the model, `p3` = the hard baseline clock + HAR-X + last-30-minute return + day-so-far range, `p0` = HAR-X + clock), scored rows only (expanding ridge, refit every 5 days, warm-up 120 days), so each prediction was made before its hour and from information up to the decision minute only.
- Window match (the forecast is for the HOUR after a decision, so a trade only uses a forecast that still covers it): a trade entered at minute m after the 09:30 open uses decision D in {60, 120, 210} (10:30, 11:30, 13:00) if D <= m < D + 60, else the trade is EXCLUDED. D = 300 (14:30) matches no tell entry (the tell's window ends at 14:30). Entry minute = (end_sec - 55800) / 60 (`tell_replay_lib.py`).
- Counts known before any outcome (entry times and dates only; the outcome columns were not loaded): 1,760 trades on 488 dates; 752 excluded (entered before 10:30 or in 12:30-13:00 or at 14:00+, no covering forecast); 729 matched and scored on 305 dates (D=60: 358, D=120: 222, D=210: 149), 2023-11-02 to 2025-05-16. The cell of interest is therefore 57 percent of the day-time of the tell, NOT its early 10:00-10:30 trades, which the model cannot speak to.

## 3. The split (no static threshold, hr-003, no look-ahead hr-020)
For each matched trade, the forecast value = p2 of its decision row. RANK = the percentile of that value among the p2 values of the SAME decision minute on strictly EARLIER scored days (expanding, causal; the first 40 scored days of each decision minute only seed the ranks and their trades are dropped from the test, count printed). WIDE = rank in the top third, NARROW = bottom third, MIDDLE = the rest (thirds are the definition of a tercile, not a tuned cut). Ranking within the decision minute removes the clock effect, so the three groups have the same time-of-day mix by construction (checked and printed).

## 4. Counted tests (3), mean book-A dollars per trade, day-block bootstrap (resample dates, 4,000 draws, seed 20261105), 98.33 percent CI (Bonferroni 3), detectable effect (2.8 x SE) PRINTED FIRST
- **T1:** mean(WIDE) minus mean(NARROW).
- **T2 (the right control, hr-041, same pass and method):** the same contrast built on the hard baseline `p3` (ranked and split the same way), and the DIFFERENCE of the two contrasts, paired by trade. Does the model add anything the persistence terms do not? A T1 effect that the baseline reproduces is volatility persistence, not the model.
- **T3 (the simulated approve/veto view):** per DAY dollars of APPROVED (WIDE + MIDDLE trades) minus ALL matched trades, i.e. what the user would have seen vetoing the NARROW third. Paired by date, day-block CI. T3 is mathematically tied to T1 and is the chart's own number, so it is counted for honesty rather than as independent evidence.
- **Placebo (for T1):** circularly shift the forecast series within each decision minute by a random offset of at least 10 scored days (1,000 draws), re-rank, recompute the contrast; the real T1 must lie beyond the 97.5th percentile of the placebo distribution.
- **Chronological halves rule:** the point estimates of T1 must have the same sign in both halves of the scored dates (split at the median date). A LEAD needs: T1 CI excludes zero, beats the placebo, same sign in both halves, and T2 point estimate of the same sign. Otherwise NOT SHOWN.

## 5. Reported, not counted

- By decision minute (D=60 / 120 / 210), by year, and by the share of days with more than one trade.
- Books B and C, `p0` and the clock-only split, and the same contrast with the realized next-hour range ('oracle') as the ceiling of what a perfect range forecast could do for the tell.
- The three cumulative-dollar curves (ALL / APPROVED / VETOED, trade order by date) as a table for the chart; the chart itself is built ONLY if the verdict is LEAD (section 8).

## 6. Honest limits, stated now
- Replay on the explore era; the model's features were chosen on this era (the walk-forward only removes the fitting look-ahead). The tell's explore trades have been read in many earlier tests (not sealed). There are no model predictions for 2025-05 to 2026-10 (no tick extraction), so no sealed hold-out exists for this question: the best result is a LEAD, never a confirmation.
- 43 percent of the tell's explore trades (the first 30 minutes and the gaps) get no forecast; a LEAD says nothing about them.
- Wide hours change both the wins and the losses; a bigger dollar contrast may only mean a wider range multiplies whatever edge the tell has. The model predicts size, not direction (the direction tests all failed).
- Fills are the shadow books' fills (a research model of fills, not the broker's).
- The meta-labeler chart is a veto of real trades by a model trained on them; this is a forecast of the market used as a veto, a different object and not comparable in size of effect.

## 9. RESULT (2026-11-04 night; script `scripts/tell_context/research_20261103/range_tell_filter.py`; output `scratch/range_tell_filter_20261105_output.txt`, curves `scratch/range_tell_filter_20261105/curves.csv`; look 178; claim `range_model_tell_filter_not_shown_20261105`)
Run exactly as section 4 specified. After the 40-day rank seeding: 680 trades on 273 dates (WIDE 338, MIDDLE 225, NARROW 117; the thirds are uneven because the forecast drifts up over the era and each rank is against earlier days only). Detectable effects (2.8 x SE, printed first): T1 $38.5 per trade, T2 $22.4, T3 $13.4 per day.
- **T1** WIDE minus NARROW: **-4.24 per trade [-37.75, +27.96]** (placebo 97.5th percentile +23.79, not beaten; halves +1.85 / -6.44, opposite signs).
- **T2** the same contrast on the hard baseline: -11.55; model minus baseline +7.31 [-12.13, +26.25] (sign differs from T1).
- **T3** approved minus all, per day: **-3.40 [-15.09, +8.00]**; per trade approved +0.55, all +1.82, vetoed NARROW +7.94.
- **Verdict: NOT SHOWN** (CI includes zero, placebo not beaten, halves disagree, T2 sign differs). No test is near the detectable floor in the hoped direction.
Reported, not counted: book A mean / win rate by group: WIDE +3.7 / 23.7 percent (avg win 192, avg loss -55, worst -522), MIDDLE -4.2 / 29.8 percent, NARROW +7.9 / 34.2 percent (the narrow hour wins MORE OFTEN but smaller, the wide hour wins less often but bigger: the two cancel in dollars, cv-026); book B WIDE-NARROW -25.5, book C +10.2; p0 -4.5; by decision minute 10:30 -18.7, 11:30 +12.1, 13:00 +1.1; by year 2024 -1.9, 2025 -6.1. READING: the range forecast does not sort the tell's trades by dollars; the tell earns about the same in expected-wide and expected-narrow hours.
