# Pre-registration (explore): does our day-width forecast beat, or add to, the standard HAR volatility benchmark? (step A/B of `docs/WIDTH_SCALED_STOPS_SHADOW_SCOPE_20261104.md`; register row 4; written 2026-11-04 BEFORE any HAR number was computed)

Origin: user, 2026-11-04 ('Yes lets start with the first one' = the HAR-style volatility benchmark of my ranked ideas); DeepSeek round U criticism 1 (the right control is the standard persistence model, not flat ADR). Research only, nothing live, explore only (the 522 sessions; the forecast's hold-out stays UNSPENT). Ledger family `har_width_benchmark_20261104`, counted tests 2.
## 1. Target and data (identical to the day forecast, `daytype_forecast.py` T2)
y = log RANGE RATIO = ln(RTH range / ADR), ADR = the median RTH range of the prior 20 sessions. The session table (features, ADR, range) comes from `daytype_forecast.py --dump` (an additive flag; the forecast's own numbers are unchanged). All models: expanding walk-forward, refit every 5 sessions, scored from session index 120 of the table AFTER dropping its first 22 rows (the monthly HAR window needs 22 prior sessions), so every model is fitted and scored on the same sessions.
## 2. Models
M0 BASELINE (the forecast's own): weekday dummies + prior-day range ratio (ridge, the forecast's penalty selection). M1 HAR (Corsi): OLS of y on ln(range of day t-1 / ADR_t), the mean of that over t-1..t-5, and over t-1..t-22 (the daily / weekly / monthly realized-range components; the range is the volatility proxy). M2 HAR-X: M1 plus ln(overnight range / ADR) (the overnight is known at 09:30). M3 STAGE 0 (the forecast under test): the full stage-0 feature set as in the day forecast, ridge. M4 HAR-X + STAGE 0: M2's three-plus-one terms and all stage-0 features, ridge.
## 3. Counted tests (2) and the reading
T1 (does our forecast beat the standard benchmark?): paired squared-error loss(M2) - loss(M3), positive = the stage-0 forecast is better. T2 (does the stage-0 information add to the benchmark?): loss(M2) - loss(M4), positive = adding it helps. Each: week-block bootstrap CI at 97.5 percent two-sided (Bonferroni 2, 2,000 draws), both chronological halves, and for T2 a circular-shift placebo of the stage-0 columns (200 draws, the real run's penalties reused), the placebo gate = the 97.5th percentile of the zero-centred placebo improvements. LEAD only if the CI lower bound is above 0, both halves are positive and (T2) the placebo gate is beaten. The detectable improvement (2.8 x the placebo SD, as % of the baseline loss) is printed BEFORE the verdicts.
## 4. Printed, uncounted
Each model's loss as a percent of M0 and of M2; M1 vs M0 (is HAR itself better than the forecast's baseline? this decides which baseline the width-scaled-stop controls must use); the implied stop spread: the standard deviation of exp(prediction) across scored sessions for M2 and M3 relative to their mean (how much a stop scaled by each would actually vary: DeepSeek's about-10-percent point checked); a parity line reproducing the day forecast's T2 improvement of M3 over M0 on this sample (the original +10.7 percent was on a slightly different start).
## 5. What a result means
If M3 does not beat M2 and M4 does not beat M2, the day-width forecast adds nothing beyond a standard HAR model and the width-scaled-stop idea (and its forward arms) is retired at once; the forecast stays a display and HAR-X becomes the width reference. If M4 beats M2, the stage-0 features carry something HAR does not, and the scaled-stop test (scope section 1B) uses M4's forecast and the M2 forecast as its control. Limits: explore only; range is the only volatility proxy here (no realized variance from ticks); the 22-session drop shortens the scored span; HAR is linear in logs by design.
