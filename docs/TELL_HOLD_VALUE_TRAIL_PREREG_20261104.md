One contract: a trade cannot be scaled out (MNQ, exactly 1 contract), so every exit is all or nothing and a tighter trail trades win rate and capture against the big runners that make the tell's profit (explore: win rate about 30 percent, average winner +133, average loss -54).

## 1. Data and the volatility scale (no fixed points, hr-003)
The same 1,760 explore entries (`scripts/amt_vbars/trades_V1_explore.csv`), 500-contract bars of the tell's own clock, V1 management unchanged (stop, 120-minute no-break exit, tell-fade after the break, 15:55 flatten, stop fills at its price minus a tick, a stop already beyond the market fills at the open). SCALE s = the median high-low range of the 20 completed bars ending at the signal bar (the tell's own typical-bar size; known at the fire). Every distance below is a multiple of s.

## 2. Descriptive (not counted): how far back is normal
For every trade, along its V1 life: the maximum favourable excursion (MFE), the largest pullback from the running best before the final best was reached ('pre-peak pullback') and over the whole life, all in points and in units of s; the capture ratio = exit points / MFE (MFE above 0); the share of the MFE the tell keeps overall (sum of exit points / sum of MFE); all split by outcome class (net above 0 / not) and for the top quarter of MFE (the runners). This answers 'is a pullback of this size normal on the way to a big move?': a trail tighter than the runners' typical pre-peak pullback cuts them.

## 3. The trail arms (the three counted tests)
Stop = the tighter of the tell's own stop and (best favourable price since entry minus k x s), k in {3, 6, 12}; updated after each completed bar and effective from the NEXT bar (no lookahead); everything else exactly V1; the arm can only exit earlier than V1 (its stop is never looser). Re-simulated with `watcher_stop_lib.run_dyn` (new optional `trail_k`, `scale` arguments; with the defaults it reproduces V1; parity with the file printed first, must be 0.0000).
- **Primary outcome (counted):** paired difference arm minus V1 in CAPTURE = exit points / V1's MFE (trades with V1 MFE above 0), day-block bootstrap CI at 98.33 percent (Bonferroni 0.05 / 3, 4,000 draws, seed 20261104), two-sided; predicted sign positive.
- **Guard (counted with the same verdict):** paired difference in net dollars per trade must not be below 0 beyond its detectable effect: an arm 'KEEPS MORE' only if the capture difference has its CI lower bound above 0 AND the net-dollar difference has its CI lower bound above minus (2.8 x its own SE) AND both chronological halves have a positive capture difference. If capture rises but net dollars fall significantly (CI upper bound below 0) the verdict is 'KEEPS MORE, COSTS RUNNERS' (a payoff-shape change, not an edge).
- **Reported with every arm (not counted):** win rate, average winner and loser, worst loss, exit mix, dollars saved on trades where the arm beat V1 versus dollars cut on trades where it lost to V1, the runner-cut rate (trades with V1 net above 0 where the arm exited earlier at a lower price), by entry hour (10:00-10:59 vs later), the detectable effect for both measures.

## 4. What this cannot show (stated before the run)

A positive result is a candidate for a forward shadow trail book (like the stop books), never a change to the live tell; the open trade of 2026-11-04 is not part of this window.

### A1. Amendment (2026-11-04, before any trail outcome was computed; a robustness fix, not a loosening)
The per-trade capture ratio explodes when a trade's best move is tiny (a -20 exit over a 0.5-point MFE is -40), so the counted measure is `capture = exit points / max(V1 MFE, s)`: the denominator is floored at one typical bar s (the data's own scale, not a fixed number). The aggregate share kept (sum of exit points / sum of V1 MFE) is reported alongside as a descriptive.
