## Question
Two separate questions on the live expansion rule (ROC10 onset, |Z| > 2, enter the next bar's open, stop 2.5 x EMA10(TR) + 1 tick, 30-bar hold, cost 2.0 pt; `scripts/vacuum_sim/v2.py`, `depth_tell2.trade_exit`):
- A (row 29, context): does a thrust that comes AFTER an already-extended move in its own direction do worse than other thrusts (a climax, not a continuation)? Is skipping it, or fading it, better?
- B (row 28, exit): on thrusts that are NOT extended, does a ratcheting stop beat the fixed 30-bar hold?

## Definitions (frozen; all causal at the signal bar)
- Event = ROC10 expansion onset exactly as the live detector (`scripts/vacuum_sim/features.py` + `v2.py`), direction = sign of ROC10. Events are evaluated LOCK-FREE first (every event scored independently); a production-lock pass (one position per track, as live) is run second and is a verdict condition (below).
- Extension E_t = dir x (Close_t - Close at 240 TRADING minutes earlier) / ADR20, where trading minutes skip the 17:00-18:00 ET break and the series is the continuous NQ minute series (`price_bars_primary`, both bounds on every query, cv-009). ADR20 = mean RTH high-low range of the 20 prior sessions (the repo definition used in the illustration). E_t is UNDEFINED when fewer than 240 trading minutes of the same trading day precede the signal (first four hours after the 18:00 open): those events are excluded from A and B and their count is printed.
- EXTENDED = E_t in the top tercile of E_t over strictly EARLIER defined events of the same track (expanding window, at least 30 earlier events, else the event is a SEED: it stays in the live book, is not scored in A/B, and the seed count is printed). No fixed ADR cutoff (the 0.75 ADR figure suggested by Argon was chosen after seeing 0.87 vs <= 0.57 on one session and is rejected, hr-003).
- Ratchet (arm R): initial stop = signal close - dir x 2.5 x EMA10(TR) at the signal bar (+1 tick), then after every bar close the stop moves to the running favourable extreme (high for longs, low for shorts) - dir x the same distance and never moves back; a bar whose open is beyond the stop fills at that open (gap-aware, cv-030); no time exit; hard flat at the session end (RTH 15:55, Globex 09:29:50). Cost 2.0 pt. Hold arm (H): the live rule (stop at entry, 30-bar hold, hard flat). Third arm (U, descriptive only): the user's signal exits as coded in `depth_tell2.trade_exit` (hook, zero cross, structural failure).
- Fade arm (F, descriptive only): the mirrored trade on EXTENDED events (opposite direction, same stop distance, same 30-bar hold, cost 2.0).

## Counted tests (4; Holm, family alpha 0.10, one-sided; day-blocked bootstrap, whole days resampled, 4,000 draws, seeded)
- A-RTH, A-GBX: paired difference d_i = P&L(skip-when-extended) - P&L(H) = -P&L_H,i if EXTENDED else 0, mean over ALL scored events (skipped trades counted at zero, so trading less cannot pass by itself).
- B-RTH, B-GBX: paired difference d_i = P&L(R) - P&L(H) over scored NON-extended events (bottom two terciles).
Argon proposed 2 counted tests; the tracks behave differently (hr-039), so each is run per track (4 tests). The deviation is stated here, hr-035.

## Controls and PASS rule (per test; all must hold)
1. N: at least 100 scored events and at least 20 distinct days (hr-008 floor is N >= 20 for any claim; this is stricter because of day clustering).
2. Raw paired mean: day-blocked CI lower bound > 0 at the Holm-adjusted level, AND the 25 percent trimmed mean > 0, AND the leave-one-session-out minimum paired mean > 0 (no single session decides, top-heavy payoffs expected for B).
3. Matched random-bar control: 1,000 draws of bars matched on session and clock hour with a random direction, with the same A or B treatment applied; the arm's paired mean must exceed the 95th percentile of the random draws. For A the random draw skips the same number of events per fire-time tercile (cf. the flush control).
4. Placebo: the same A or B treatment on the MIRRORED trades (same stop and hold geometry, opposite side); arm minus placebo CI lower bound > 0.
5. Both chronological halves of the window have a paired mean >= 0.
6. The production-lock pass has the same sign and its paired mean is not worse than 50 percent of the lock-free one (the ratchet holds a position longer and blocks later signals; that cost is real).
7. REPLICATION sign agrees (descriptive condition).
NOT SHOWN: anything else. A not-shown result PARKS the idea (co-025), it does not retire it. INCONCLUSIVE (structure): LONG and SHORT subsets have opposite-signed paired means each beyond 0.5 standard errors; reported as such. Direction splits are descriptive.

## Amendment A5 (2026-11-09 night, after Argon's round-16 feedback and my own line-by-line check of `scripts/vacuum_sim/context_exit_tests.py`; BEFORE any outcome of these arms was read; look 213-216 are the outcome-blind audits)

## Amendment A6 (2026-11-09 night, BEFORE any outcome of these arms; found by the outcome-blind null Monte Carlo of amendment A5(7), 30 iterations, family-wise PASS 3 of 30, all from test A-RTH = 10 percent for one test where about 5 percent is expected)
The pass condition "beats the 95th percentile of 1,000 matched random-bar control draws" is REPLACED by a PAIRED, DAY-BLOCKED comparison: for every scored event i, c_i = the expectation of the control statistic over ALL random bars of the same date, track and hour (fallback: same track and hour on other dates, share printed); the condition is that the day-blocked bootstrap 5th percentile of mean(y_i - c_i) is above zero (test A: control statistic = -P&L_H of the random bar; test B: ratchet minus hold of the random bar). Reason: the old comparison drew only from a FIXED control pool and ignored the pool's own sampling noise, so the observed mean (a fresh sample) exceeded its 95th percentile more often than 5 percent (theory about 12 percent: the difference of two independent sampling errors); the paired day-blocked form carries both. The mirrored placebo for test A and every other gate are unchanged. The null Monte Carlo is re-run on the fixed control before the real run and its result is recorded as a claim.

## Amendment A7 (2026-11-09 night, after DeepSeek's round-16 code review `scratch/deepseek_response_round16_clean.txt`; BEFORE any outcome of these arms; audited line by line before adoption)
(1) TEST A is re-stated as a CONTRAST. ## Amendment A8 (2026-11-09 evening, after Argon's D1 review; BEFORE any outcome of these arms; Argon's criticism 3, verified)
The drop-top-2-days gate of test A (A7(1)) subtracted the two most contributing days' terms from the contrast but kept the ORIGINAL group denominators, which shrinks the contrast toward zero (a mathematically invalid "mean without those days"). It is now: rank days by their INFLUENCE (Delta minus Delta with that day left out), remove the two most influential days, RECOMPUTE both group means from the remaining events, and require the recomputed contrast above zero. (The leave-one-day-out minimum already used the correct denominators; test B's drop-top-2 is a one-sample mean with correct denominators and is unchanged.) Also noted, no rule change: the mirrored placebo of test A is, when no stop is hit, almost exactly an affine copy of the contrast (the mirrored trade's value is minus the trade's value minus the two costs), so the placebo condition is largely redundant with Delta > 0 and is NOT an independent null test; the matched random-bar control is the real guard.
