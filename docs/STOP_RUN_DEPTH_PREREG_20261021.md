# Pre-registration (stage 3): do the offers actually disappear and the bids come in at a sweep — order-book depth — 2026-10-21

Written and committed BEFORE the depth extractor is built or any depth feature or outcome is computed. Origin: the user's description of the mechanism (2026-10-21): price drives into a known level with force, then "all the offers disappear and the bids come in and lift it" (mirror for highs). Stages 1 and 2 (`UNIFIED_SWEEP_ROOM_AND_SEQUENCE_PREREG_20261021.md`) could only look at trades; offers disappearing is a resting-order-book event. Research only; nothing live changes.

## Data and what is known about it (checked, not assumed)
- Sierra Chart `.depth` files: `/mnt/c/SierraChart/Data/MarketDepthData` (about 96 UTC dates 2026-07-10 to 2026-10-13) and `/mnt/c/SierraChart-DTC/Data/MarketDepthData` (about 21 dates to 2026-10-20), 0.5-1 GB per day. Always streamed in chunks, never loaded whole.
- **Format** (Sierra's own page is permanently removed; the record layout is from the Sierra Chart support board where staff confirmed 64-byte header and 24-byte record, and was verified against a real file before this was written): header `SCDD`, header size 64, record size 24, version 1, then records `<int64 datetime, int8 command, int8 flags, int16 orders, float32 price, uint32 quantity, uint32 reserved>`; datetime = microseconds since 1899-12-30 UTC; price stored times 100; flag 1 = end of a batch of updates; file date = UTC date. Command codes were NOT documented anywhere reachable; the observed pattern (command 1 appears first with no price; 2 and 3 appear as price ladders descending from the best bid and ascending from the best ask; 4/5 and 6/7 are most frequent afterwards) gives the working hypothesis: 1 clear book, 2 insert bid, 3 insert ask, 4 update bid, 5 update ask, 6 delete bid, 7 delete ask. The test below treats this as a hypothesis to be proven.
- **Validation gate (decides whether any result is reported):** the rebuilt book must (a) be crossed (best bid at or above best ask) in at most 1 percent of RTH seconds, and (b) contain the last trade price of each second (from the per-second price archive `scratch/persec_px_20261021`) between best bid minus one tick and best ask plus one tick in at least 98 percent of RTH seconds. A session failing either is excluded and counted; if fewer than 60 sessions pass, or the command hypothesis fails, no depth result is reported and the failure is the finding.

## Extraction
`scripts/depth_persec_20261021.mjs` streams each file, applies the commands to bid and ask ladders indexed by tick (0.25 point), and at every second of the RTH session (09:30:00-16:00:00 ET, UTC converted with the correct daylight-saving offset for the date) writes: best bid, best ask, size at best bid, size at best ask, total bid size and total ask size within 10 ticks (2.5 points) and within 40 ticks (10 points) of the best on each side. Output `scratch/depth_persec_20261021/DATE.tsv`.

## Events and outcomes
Population: the joined sweep replay events (`scratch/unified_sweep_events_20261021.json`, thinned to one event per direction per 10 minutes, same rule as the sensitivity run) on sessions with a valid depth file (2026-07-10 to 2026-10-20); outcomes = hold-to-15:58 dollars and the k = 3 R-race at b = 1.0 (stop 1.0 x the typical 10-minute range beyond the extreme), with the flipped-direction placebo already stored. Secondary, descriptive only: the real RTH-fired STOP_SWEEP and FAILED_SWEEP_REVERSAL rows in the same window, winners versus losers on the same features.
Split by date, frozen now: explore = events to 2026-08-31, hold-out = 2026-09-01 to 2026-10-20 (the depth window is only about 3.5 months, so the earlier years' split cannot be used).

## Features (point-in-time; depth snapshot at the event time T = end of the event minute; extreme second as in stage 2)
For a long the drive is down: the "offers" are the ask side and the "bids" the bid side; for a short mirror.
- **A thin offers:** total ask size within 10 ticks at T divided by the median of that quantity over the 120 seconds before the extreme second. Tight = at or below the explore median.
- **B bids come in:** total bid size within 10 ticks at T divided by the median of that quantity over the same 120 seconds. Tight = at or above the explore median.
- **I imbalance at T:** (bid size minus ask size within 10 ticks) / (their sum), aligned to the trade (a long: positive = bids heavier). Tight = at or above the explore median.
- **S sequence:** all three tight. (Held-out cut: explore medians frozen for the hold-out.)
A 40-tick version of A, B, I is computed and reported as a sensitivity only.

## Tests (frozen, same rules as stage 2)
H1 A, H2 B, H3 I, H4 S, outcome hold dollars and k = 3 R-race at b = 1.0: tight minus loose, day-blocked bootstrap 95 percent interval of the difference, explore and hold-out separately. KEPT (strong) = both intervals above zero; KEPT (weak) = explore above zero and hold-out point estimate positive; else NOT SHOWN. MECHANISM SUPPORTED only if a hypothesis is kept AND its hold-out tight subset beats its flipped-direction version with the interval above zero, with at least 20 hold-out events on 20 distinct days; otherwise NOT SHOWN, stated as what could not be shown. 4 hypotheses x 2 outcomes x 2 periods = 16 intervals (about one spurious favourable expected). Also reported with no verdict: the typical timeline of the ask-side and bid-side near-touch size from 120 s before to 60 s after the extreme second, for tight versus loose events and for real winners versus losers.

## Known limits (stated in advance)
Sierra's depth feed shows displayed size by price level only (no order-level data, no hidden or iceberg orders) and the number of levels recorded is limited by the data provider; the depth window is about 3.5 months; the replay events only approximate the live rules; near-touch liquidity changes for many reasons unrelated to stops; the command-code mapping is empirically inferred (gate above); the user's mechanism may depend on features outside this feed.

## Addendum (written after the first extraction failed the validation gate, before ANY depth feature or outcome was computed)
The first extraction (77 dates, one file per date chosen by size) failed the gate as written: median share of seconds with the last trade inside the rebuilt book was 91.7 percent against the 98 percent required, zero sessions passed. Diagnosis with no outcomes involved (`scratch/depth_diag_20261021.mjs`): (1) timestamps are aligned exactly (agreement peaks at a zero-second shift, 93 percent, and falls to 58 percent at one second); (2) the strict criterion compares the book at the END of a second with the LAST trade inside it, so a book that moved within a fast second looks wrong although the trade range overlaps it (98.0-98.5 percent of seconds on the sessions checked); (3) two reader defects: the DTC-install files (`NQZ26.*`) store prices unscaled while the other install stores them times 100 (best bid 297.5 instead of 29750), and on contract-roll dates there are two contract files and the larger was picked even when the trades were in the other. Amendments, all about the reader and made before any outcome exists: the price scale is detected per file (median raw price above one million = stored times 100, else unscaled); on a date with more than one candidate file every candidate is extracted and the one that validates is used; the validation criterion becomes: the second's traded range [low, high] from the per-second price archive overlaps [best bid minus one tick, best ask plus one tick] in at least 98 percent of RTH seconds, and the book is crossed in at most 1 percent of seconds (the original last-trade figures are still reported). Dates 2026-10-20 (no price archive yet) are excluded from validation and from the analysis. The rest of the pre-registration (features, split, tests, rules) is unchanged. If fewer than 60 sessions still pass, no depth result is reported and the failure is the finding.
