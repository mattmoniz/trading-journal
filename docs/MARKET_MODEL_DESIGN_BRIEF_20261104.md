# Design brief: a market-reading model, built around the few levers that decide whether it can work (2026-11-04; NOT BUILT; for a constructive design review)

Audience: the reviewer (Opus, high effort). The user does NOT want every hole poked; they want the right levers pulled so a model has a fair chance. Please spend your effort on the 4-5 choices that most determine success, say what to do, and flag a problem only if it is fatal.

## 1. What the user wants (their words, 2026-11-04)
'Something that analyzes where price is now in the trading day at any moment, looks at order flow, structure and volume profile, compares it to where it has been over 5 days and maybe 20 days and where it has spent most of the time, makes calls from it, and sees if it learns something we cannot ... I also don't know what the exit would be.' Then: 'Will it detect support and resistance, absorption and exhaustion? ... can we just give it all the structure, order flow and tick data and let it build a picture? Are there tools to help?' and 'should we have a separate model for every specific factor?' (answer given: no, factors are input groups, models are split by question).

## 2. Constraints that do not move
One MNQ contract ($2 per point, $2 round trip, ground truth `server/config/instruments.js`). Shadow only first: no orders. No lookahead; every feature known at the decision time. About 520 explore sessions (2023-03 to 2025-05, 500-contract bar files and per-minute tick data) and a later sealed hold-out (2025-05 to 2026-10, one look only). Prop-account goal: a 'pass race' (volatility control matters as much as edge). RTH and Globex are separate problems (signals that hold in RTH can reverse overnight). Static thresholds are not allowed: cut-offs come from the data's own rolling distribution. Realistic fills only (a stop beyond the market fills at the next open).

## 3. What the evidence so far says (this matters more than any architecture)
REAL, about MAGNITUDE: (1) a plain HAR-X volatility model (yesterday's, 5-day and 22-day realized range plus the overnight range) improves the day-width forecast by 14 percent (CI excludes 0); our own richer day-shape forecast added no detectable increment beyond it. (2) The clock (time-of-day activity) carries weight. (3) The rotation count by midnight (a volatile-regime marker): the overnight flow-sign trade made about +239 per trade on the 2026 nights with 6+ rotations (CI +99..+365), beat a momentum control, but is rare and mostly 2026. (4) On normal initial-balance-extension days a wider stop paid about 13 dollars per trade better (CI above 0). (5) The tell's node-ladder structure predicted a break of the range (counted primary narrowly missed), not its direction.
NULL (tested, explore, pre-registered, day-block): direction from the volume-profile shape letter, delta/effort/volume readings at a stall, volume per point, footprint diagonal imbalances and stacks (per-bar, 500v to 10,000v bars), overnight inventory vs the RTH open, the rotation VWAP as a filter, balance-vs-imbalance forecasting, the meta-labeler (test AUC 0.55-0.57, out-of-sample approved vs vetoed about 0.56 dollars per trade). Pattern: inputs predict HOW MUCH the market moves; direction has been null almost everywhere except the narrow overnight regime.

## 4. The proposed design (small, by question, not by factor)
MODEL 1, RANGE AND EXPANSION (highest prior): at every 15-minute decision point in RTH, predict the remaining range today and the probability of an extension beyond the initial balance in the next hour. Baseline = HAR-X + clock; every other input must earn its place as an increment (drop-one-group test). Uses: size stops and targets, flag stay-out conditions.
MODEL 2, LEVEL-TOUCH REACTION: event dataset = every touch of a structural level (IB high/low, overnight high/low, prior-day high/low/POC/VAH/VAL, composite 5-day and 20-day POC/VAH/VAL and volume nodes, single-print edges). Label = triple barrier sized by the day's expected width (reverse by k width before break by k, 60-minute time limit), outcome in dollars after fills. Inputs AT the touch: approach speed, delta now-vs-lately, effort per point, per-bar imbalance counts, bar speed, times the level was touched before and what happened (level strength from the last 5 and 20 days), distance to the next node, day state (balance / drive, IB state). This is where 'support and resistance, absorption, exhaustion' would be learned: as patterns at the touch, with the touch outcome as the teacher.
MODEL 3, the existing overnight flow-sign regime flag (rare, 2026): unchanged, a regime switch for the one directional edge.
Feature GROUPS (not separate models): A history (5-day / 20-day composite value, range position, prior-day and overnight levels, unfinished business), B time at price (volume-at-price nodes, share of today's time near the current price), C order flow (up/down-tick delta, bid/ask imbalance, effort, tape speed; always now-vs-lately), D structure (IB state, rotation legs and VWAP, balance vs drive, range so far / expected), E context (HAR-X width, overnight range, gap, weekday).
Learner: regularized linear model first, gradient boosting (LightGBM, already in the repo) as the challenger; no deep sequence model unless the boosted model shows signal. Evaluation: expanding walk-forward with purging and an embargo for overlapping labels, sample weights by label uniqueness, day-block bootstrap, drop-one-group ablation and SHAP for 'what did it find', a probability-of-backtest-overfitting number, every model scored against the baselines (always flat, time-of-day mean, HAR-X) in DOLLARS after fills. Hold-out: sealed, one look. Then forward SHADOW on the Monitor and Learning page (one section, with its own written next steps and retire rule).
EXITS: for Model 2 events, fixed by design: stop and target scaled by the expected width (Model 1's output), plus a 60-minute time limit; a model-managed exit (re-score every few minutes, leave when expected remaining value is negative) only if the fixed-exit version shows a call worth managing.

## 5. Data on disk

## 7. A1. Opus (high effort) constructive review, relayed 2026-11-04 night (a subagent report, not a user decision; NOTHING below is adopted until the user says so)
Levers, ranked: (1) give Model 1 (how much the market will move) a trade of its own that does not need a direction: a two-sided breakout BRACKET (a buy stop just above and a sell stop just below the recent range, one cancels the other, stop and target sized by the expected width): 'the most important choice in the design'; (2) few decision times about 60-90 minutes apart (10:30, 11:30, 13:00, 14:30) with non-overlapping labels, about 4 clean examples a day, the DAY as the unit for every interval (about 520 days, not thousands of rows); (3) a width-unit label fixed before looking: next 60-minute range / expected width (HAR-X day width times the clock's typical share for that hour), and a yes/no version 'moves at least k x width beyond the range so far in 60 minutes' with k set once from the first year (base rate about 30-40 percent) then frozen, a cost floor under any barrier (hr-044); (4) features in order of prior strength, each judged as an increment: baseline HAR-X + clock, then group D (range so far / expected, IB width / expected, rotation count), then the 'now vs lately' part of group C (last-30-minute realized range and volume vs the 20-day norm for the same time of day); defer history, time-at-price and footprint imbalances (location / direction inputs, already null); volume only from the tick-derived files (hr-070); (5) one simple evaluation: expanding walk-forward in quarterly blocks, ridge first, baseline compared on the same rows, day-block intervals and the circular-shift placebo, one pre-registered primary metric, a calibration check, then the money test in dollars after realistic fills.
Cut or defer: Model 2 (level-touch reaction: 'reverse before break' is a direction call in disguise, build only after Model 1 passes, feed it Model 1's expansion probability, merge nearby levels into zones, one event per zone per touch episode, cap per day), SHAP, probability of backtest overfitting, uniqueness weights, purging (not needed with non-overlapping labels), LightGBM, the remaining-range target, model-managed exits. Keep Model 3 unchanged. First build: Model 1, next-hour expansion, RTH only. Cheapest experiment (about a day, data on disk: `rth_1m_flow.json`, `daytype_forecast_table.csv`): STEP A ridge of log(next-hour range / expected) at the four times on baseline vs baseline + D + C-now-vs-lately (GO: beats the placebo gate, interval lower bound above 0, both halves positive; STOP: HAR-X is the only 'how much' information, use it for stop sizing without a model); STEP B only if A passes: simulate the bracket on the model's top 20 percent against the always-on bracket and the HAR-X-only bracket, realistic stop-entry fills and the $2 round trip (GO: more dollars per trade than both controls, day-block lower bound above 0). Continue-or-stop: continue only if both pass on explore; else stop building models, keep HAR-X for stop sizing, record the null; the sealed hold-out is spent once, only on a version that passed both. Fatal problems: none. Most likely mistake: spending the effort on Model 2 and reading AUC 0.55-0.57 as signal while the one predictable thing (how far the market moves) never becomes a trade that pays without knowing direction. ## 8. A2. Opus on ORDER-BOOK DEPTH (relayed 2026-11-04 night; a subagent report, NOT adopted until the user says so)
Keep depth OUT of the model for now and log it FORWARD-ONLY. Why not now: the model trains on 2023-2025 and there is no depth from those years (a feature with no training data cannot be learned or tested), and the depth sessions (2026-06-15 onward) sit inside the sealed hold-out era, so mining them for depth ideas would spend the hold-out without saying so; clean exploration data starts after the hold-out period ends, about November 2026 onward. How to log: a small row at each of Model 1's fixed decision times (10:30, 11:30, 13:00, 14:30) and, if Model 2 is ever built, at each level touch, from the per-second summariser (`scripts/depth_persec_20261021.mjs`): resting size at the best price and within 10 and 40 ticks each side, the bid-vs-ask imbalance, and how fast the side being approached thinned or refilled over the last 1-5 minutes; every number NOW-VERSUS-LATELY against its own rolling 20-day norm for the same time of day, no fixed cut-offs; the row written before the outcome and labelled later by the same rules; keep the per-second summaries, the raw 1 GB a day is optional. Where it is most likely to help: as a HOW-MUCH input for Model 1 (a thin book near price, a bigger next-hour move), not a direction input (the earlier sweep / absorption tests were direction calls and were null). The six-pivot look is an idea, not evidence (the pivots were picked because they turned; the real test compares touches that held with touches that broke at the same kind of level). Sessions before trusting a feature: about 120 fresh sessions (about 6 months) for ONE pre-registered depth add-on to HAR-X + clock + the intraday groups (the HAR test used 356 sessions and could only detect about 4 percent, so expect a rough answer), about 250 sessions (a full year) before it enters a live model and only if the 120-session test passed; if depth adds nothing at 250 sessions, stop recording raw depth and record the null.

## 10. A4. Opus (high effort) REVISED review with the two failed brackets in hand (relayed 2026-11-04 night; a subagent report, NOT adopted until the user says so)
The bracket recommendation is WITHDRAWN. Recommendation: PARK the market-model project as a trade generator (no Model 1, no Model 2). 'Magnitude is predictable' is a SWITCH for when to look for direction, not a trade: the one directional edge lives inside a high-activity regime (Opus's reading of the overnight results: flow sign on all 863 nights hit rate 49 percent and about +30 per trade, on nights with 6 or more rotation legs +239 per trade): the regime picks the moment, the flow picks the side. Keep two cheap items: the HAR-X-scaled stop test on the overnight trade (register row 4, waits for the user's go) and Step A as statistics only (only for stay-out flags). RANKED DIRECTIONAL IDEAS: (1) the overnight mechanism moved into RTH, only in the high-rotation regime (qualify a day when its rotation-leg count by 11:00 is at or above the expanding 90th percentile of earlier days; enter at 11:00 in the sign of the 09:30-11:00 up-tick minus down-tick volume; stop about one HAR-X width; exit 15:55; controls on the same days: random direction, price-momentum sign, always long, plus the qualifying-minus-other-days contrast; data `scratch/updown_1m_20261104/`, `rotationDetector.js`, 1-minute RTH bars; prior low to moderate because the overnight effect is mostly late 2025 to 2026 and explore ends 2025-05; BEFORE looking at any outcome count the qualifying explore days and do not run it below about 40, track it forward instead); (2) the tell as DIRECTION CONTEXT for other setups (register row 1: split the 9,942 shadow fires by whether they agree with the active tell side at the fire; day-block CI, both halves, always-long drift control; no new data; prior low to moderate); (3) calendar flows (turn of the month, the day before FOMC; daily bars; about 100 and about 18 explore days, so only a large effect is detectable; prior low; costs an hour); (4) NQ vs ES catch-up (a pre-registration exists, `NQ_ES_DIVERGENCE_REVERSION_PREREG_20261026`, no look was ever run, ES bars loaded; prior low).
