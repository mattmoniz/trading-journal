## Definitions (causal)
Population TELL = the V1 simulated signal bars (1,760), direction d = the tell direction. CONTROL = decision-grid bars (every 10th index, 10:00-14:30) with price moving and no tell, direction = sign of the last 10-bar move (the tell's own preceding-move rule). Stop distance s = k x the median (high - low) of the last 20 bars at the signal bar (data-derived, scale-free; typical 7-14 points), k in {1, 2}.
Leg 1: entry at the OPEN of bar i+1 plus one tick adverse slip in direction d; stop at entry - d x s (a bar opening beyond the stop fills at the open, one tick adverse); exits: the stop, a 120-minute time exit measured from the signal bar's end (as `trade.stripped_full`), or the 15:55 open; no profit target. Variant A (no reversal): one leg only. Variant B (reversal): when a leg is stopped out at bar j (STOP or STOP_GAP), enter the OPPOSITE direction at the open of bar j+1 plus slip with the same distance s and a fresh 120-minute clock; at most 2 reversals (3 legs in total); a time or end-of-day exit ends the sequence. Net per signal = the sum of its legs' dollars (costs per leg).
## Counted tests (family `tell_stop_reverse_20261102`, ledger tests = 4, BH q = 0.10)
Cells k = 1 and k = 2 x variants A and B. For each: the TELL net per signal minus the CONTROL net per signal (day-blocked bootstrap CI, 3000 draws), plus the TELL absolute mean per signal with its CI. CANDIDATE = BH survivor, difference CI excludes zero, TELL absolute mean CI lower bound above zero, same sign in both chronological halves, at least 1,000 TELL signals on at least 300 dates. Uncounted: legs per signal, stop-out share, mean per leg, the original V1 net per trade (+$1.93) as the reference, by year, the reversal legs alone (does the reversal direction beat the first leg).
Resolution, stated now: about 1,700 TELL signals with a per-signal SD of about $60 resolve a mean of about +/-$3; a cost-sized effect (a few dollars per signal) is only barely visible.
