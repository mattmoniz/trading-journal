# Design review — `FLOOR_ENTRY_ES_AGREEMENT_PREREG_20261026.md`

1. **E2 cannot isolate ES (primary contrast, most serious).** AGREE/DISAGREE is stamped on the fire bar — the same bar NQ turns — and NQ–ES correlate 0.916 at lag 0, so DISAGREE is a small, atypical subset where NQ moved but ES lagged/diverged. That subset differs in NQ's own move quality (idiosyncratic pop, weaker/slower turn), not only in ES state. AGREE−DISAGREE therefore measures "NQ turn with vs without ES participation," confounded with NQ turn strength, and cannot answer "does ES agreeing add value." *Fix:* match AGREE/DISAGREE on NQ fire-bar covariates (turn magnitude in A15, bars-to-turn), or reframe as a lead/lag contrast (ES turned ≥1 bar earlier vs same bar vs never).

2. **E1's all-clock unconditional baseline is unfair.** A fire entry sits at a value-area low during a floor test with a confirmed turn; comparing it to "all bars at the same clock minute, all days" mixes calm and trending minutes. E1 can be SHOWN just from being at a low during an active move, not from the entry being worthwhile. *Fix:* make E3a (same delay after episode start, no turn condition) the primary baseline; demote the all-clock unconditional to descriptive.

3. **Survivorship returns via pooled K arms.** Pooling K1/K2/K3P mixes first touches with repeat touches; a floor tested k≥3 has already held repeatedly and is selected to keep holding. The floor study's Amendment 1 fixed this with a within-sequence paired contrast; pooling reopens it. *Fix:* pair E1 within-sequence (K3P minus the same sequence's K1) and gate on the paired form.

4. **Pass-bar gaps.** E1 SHOWN requires only bootstrap >0 plus both halves positive, so a delay/entry-price artifact passes (E3a is reported, not gating). E1 and E2 are two primaries with no family-wise control, each with bootstrap + shuffle + halves. *Fix:* E1 must also exceed the E3a control; evaluate E2 only if E1 passes (sequential gate) or pre-specify BH across E1/E2.

5. **Shuffle (E3b) is near-tautological when DISAGREE is rare.** With AGREE dominant, within-day relabeling barely changes composition, so the 97.5th percentile sits next to the observed statistic and power is ~0; 1000 draws over few distinct days is unstable. *Fix:* require ≥20 distinct days and a minimum DISAGREE count before testing E2; shuffle at day level; raise draws.

6. **E3a is underspecified.** Unclear whether the control episode must still be active at the sampled delay (episode ends when low > VAL+2p), which pool it samples, and whether it matches minute-of-day (E1's baseline is clock-matched). *Fix:* censor control entries to still-active episodes, sample within family/arm/trade-date set, match clock minute.

7. **No lookahead — but the definition mismeasures the claim.** ES close at the fire bar is a completed bar before entry (open of the next bar), and A15/fire thresholds use prior data only, so it is causal — good. But reading ES on the same bar NQ turns is the maximally collinear timing and cannot test the stated "ES already turning/taking off earlier" lead idea. *Fix:* add a strict-lead variant (ES turned ≥1 bar before the NQ fire) as the lead test; keep the same-bar reading secondary.

8. **SHOWN/NOT SHOWN can mislead.** E1 and E2 are collinear — AGREE dominates the fire pool, so E1 ≈ AGREE−unconditional ≈ E2's descriptive line, meaning the two "primaries" are not independent evidence. NOT SHOWN for E2 risks reading "ES adds nothing" when DISAGREE is merely underpowered; "both halves positive" can fail a real effect in the 40% holdout. *Fix:* report E1/E2 overlap and holdout n, print the smallest-detectable effect, and state the underpowered reading explicitly.
