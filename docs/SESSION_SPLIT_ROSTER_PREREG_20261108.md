# Pre-registration: RTH vs Globex split of the live roster rule (written 2026-11-08 BEFORE any outcome of this arm was computed; user: 'run the rth vs globex test next', after the five-bucket partial-pooling arm was NOT SHOWN)

STATUS: FROZEN. Research only, nothing live. The user's purpose: setups should fire only in the part of the day (here: the session) where they perform. This is the SIMPLE version of `docs/BUCKET_ROSTER_PREREG_20261108.md`: two buckets, no model, the SAME promotion rule the live roster uses, applied to a different KEY. Ledger family `session_split_roster_20261108`, 2 counted tests; candidate Q4-3 in `docs/CANDIDATE_QUEUE.md`.

## 1. The question
If the roster rule is applied per (setup type, session) instead of per setup type, are next-day results better? Session by the ET clock of `fired_at` (cv-004: not by the setup name): RTH = 09:35-16:00; GLOBEX = 18:30-24:00 and 00:00-09:30; 09:30-09:35 and 16:00-18:30 are the existing hard dead zones and belong to neither.
## 2. Arms (rebuilt AS OF each calendar day from rows resolved before that day's 00:00; same population and basis filters as `scripts/calibrate_live_roster.mjs`; scored on DECLUSTERED fires, `is_cluster_primary`, hr-007)
- **POOLED_OLD** = the live rule before 2026-11-08 (`evaluateType` + `buildRoster`, day-block 95 percent CI lower bound > 0, both halves positive, at least 5 days, new promotions survive leave-best-day-out) on the key `type`.
- **SESSION_OLD** = the identical rule on the key `type|session`; a fire is taken when its (type, session) cell is on the roster.
- Reported, not counted: **POOLED_HOLM** and **SESSION_HOLM** (the same two plus the Holm correction at family-wise 0.10 across the cells testable that day, the rule live since 2026-11-08); **ALL** (every declustered fire).
Same rule, different key: arms differ only in how the evidence is cut (cv-026). Because cutting by session halves the data per cell, SESSION_OLD is at a statistical disadvantage by construction; a win is therefore informative, a loss partly expected.
## 3. Counted tests (2), day-block bootstrap (4,000 draws, seed 20261108), 97.5 percent CI (Bonferroni 2), detectable effect (2.8 x SE) printed first
**T1:** mean daily dollars, SESSION_OLD minus POOLED_OLD (paired by calendar day, days where either arm takes a trade). **T2:** dollars PER TRADE (sum/N per arm, bootstrap over days), SESSION_OLD minus POOLED_OLD. LEAD = T1 CI lower bound above 0 AND both chronological halves positive. First scored day = the first calendar day with at least 25 distinct prior trade days.
## 4. Reported, not counted
Per arm: trades, per-trade mean, win rate, average win / loss, worst day, days with trades; by session (RTH vs Globex) for each arm; a HETEROGENEITY table for types with at least 5 days in BOTH sessions (over everything resolved, not as-of): RTH mean, Globex mean, difference, and the share of such types whose Globex mean is below the RTH mean (hr-039 expects momentum / flow types to be worse in Globex, fade types unknown); the cells SESSION_OLD takes that POOLED_OLD does not and the reverse.
## 5. Honest limits
Window 2026-07-09 onward (about 100 days; the data that suggested the question), so retrospective = a lead at most; forward re-run `--first-forward 2026-11-09` at 60 and 120 forward days is the confirmation (kill rule: no better than POOLED_OLD at both). Most types fire in one session only (the `_OVERNIGHT` types are Globex by design), so the arms can differ only for types that fire in both. Cells have few days; the old rule at 5 days has no multiplicity correction, which is exactly why the Holm variants are shown.

## 6. RESULT (2026-11-08; script `scripts/backtest_session_roster.mjs`, ledger look 185, claim `session_split_roster_20261108`)
79 scored calendar days (2026-08-07..2026-11-06), 10,265 rows, 37 active days (either arm takes a trade). Detectable effect printed first: T1 $37.2 per day.
- **T1** SESSION_OLD minus POOLED_OLD, mean daily dollars: **+27.97 [+3.59, +62.32]**; halves +16.06 / +39.26. **T2** dollars per trade: **+9.28 [+1.25, +20.44]**. **Verdict: LEAD** (retrospective, a lead at most).
- Arms (declustered fires): POOLED_OLD 109 trades, -8.14 per trade, total -887; SESSION_OLD 129 trades, +1.15 per trade, total +148; POOLED_HOLM 17 trades, -8.35; SESSION_HOLM 15 trades, +23.67 (5 active days: anecdote); ALL 6,490 trades, -2.51. SESSION_OLD took a strict superset of POOLED_OLD's fires (POOLED took nothing SESSION did not).
- **What drives it (read this before believing it):** the whole difference comes from 20 extra fires in three cells: IB_HIGH_FADE_SHORT|RTH (13 fires), CAM_S1_FADE_LONG|RTH (5), FLOOR_PIVOT_FADE_SHORT_OVERNIGHT|GLOBEX (2). By session SESSION_OLD took 115 RTH trades (-750) against POOLED_OLD's 97 (-1,429), and 14 Globex trades (+898) against 12 (+542): the 18 extra RTH fires made about +679 and the 2 extra Globex fires about +356. These types have a Globex or other-session history that dilutes their pooled evidence below the bar; cut by session the RTH cell clears it. A LEAD resting on about 20 fires is thin.
- Heterogeneity (7 types with at least 5 days in both sessions, all resolved rows, descriptive): Globex mean is below RTH for 5 of 7 (71 percent); the largest gap is GLOBEX_VWAP_FADE_LONG (RTH +15.6, Globex -16.6; the type named 'GLOBEX' does worse in Globex), the others differ by $0-8. Consistent with hr-039, not a test.
- READING: splitting the roster rule by session helped in this window, mostly by un-diluting two RTH cells; it did NOT rescue the pooled rule (SESSION_OLD is about +$1 per trade, ALL is -$2.5) and it has the same multiplicity weakness as the old rule. NOT tested: more than 2 buckets beyond the earlier five-bucket arm, forward days. The live gate (`setupEligibility.js` keys the roster by setup type only) was NOT changed. Forward confirmation: re-run `node scripts/backtest_session_roster.mjs --first-forward 2026-11-09` at 60 and 120 forward days (kill rule: no better than POOLED_OLD at both).

## 7. Side finding the same day: the bootstrap p behind the live Holm correction was seed-noisy
At 4,000 draws the Monte Carlo noise (about +/-0.0005) equals the Holm threshold (alpha / family size = 0.10 / ~200 = 0.0005): PD_SESSION_MID_FADE_SHORT_OVERNIGHT showed p 0.0012-0.0022 across seeds (0.0002 in one session-keyed run) and 0.0014 at 400,000 draws, so its pass / fail depended on the seed. Fixed in `scripts/calibrate_live_roster.mjs`: any type with a first-pass p <= 0.05 is re-estimated with 400,000 draws (`ROSTER_CORRECTION.refineB`, 6 seconds a run). Roster result unchanged: only FLOOR_R1_FADE_SHORT_OVERNIGHT (p about 0 at 400,000 draws) survives; PD_SESSION_MID (0.0014), FLOOR_PIVOT (0.0038) and CAM_S1 (0.016) fail Holm at the current family size. The walk-forward backtests still use the 4,000-draw p (an arm comparison, not a gate), a known small noise source in their Holm variants.
