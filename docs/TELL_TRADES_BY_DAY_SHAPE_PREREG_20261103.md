# Pre-registration (descriptive): what does the shape of the day (B vs D) mean for the tell's trades? (written 2026-11-03 night BEFORE any outcome of this comparison is read)

STATUS: FROZEN. Descriptive, hindsight, explore window only (2023-04..2025-05-16), uncounted for any gate; ledger family `tell_trades_by_day_shape_20261103`; nothing here changes what the system trades or sizes. Purpose (user, 2026-11-03: "How can we assess the impact of incorrectly or correctly trading a B shape vs a D shape? How much would it impact us?"): put dollar numbers on the question by splitting the tell's explore trades by the shape of the day they happened on.
## 1. Trades (all taken from existing code, no new trading logic)
- BOOK A = the tell as built: the 1,760 V1 trades of `scripts/amt_vbars/trades_V1_explore.csv` (the file the Stage-2 book is built from; mean +1.93 dollars/trade, CI [-3.44, 7.62] on the 2026-10-28 run). The live shadow-book code is re-run on each of those fires and book A must reproduce the file's net dollars (parity, reported).
- BOOK B = same direction, stop just beyond the value edge; BOOK C = the FLIP of fires labelled AGAINST (other fires follow the tell), exactly as `scripts/tell_context/shadow.py` `simulate_fire` and `context.context_at_fire` do live (fill and exit conventions are the tell's: next bar open plus one tick, 2 dollars/point, 2 dollars commission, V1 exits). Conventions, not validated.
## 2. Day-shape groups (from the final RTH profile, `scratch/tell_context_20261103/b_d_formation_rows.json`, shared `profile_shape.py`)
Final letter D / P / b; MULTI-HUMP (bimodality at or above 0.365) vs not; STRONG B (top decile of bimodality, cut 0.61) vs STRONG D (bimodality at or below 0.09 and letter D). REALISTIC ARM: the developing multi-hump flag (bimodality of the profile so far at or above 0.365) at the last 30-minute checkpoint at or before the fire's signal bar (what the system could have seen); fires before the first checkpoint count as 'not flagged'.
## 3. Contrasts (the whole counted set, tests = 10) and outputs
K1 final multi-hump vs not; K2 strong B vs strong D; K3 developing flag at fire vs not (books A, B, C each = 9), K4 book C only, restricted to AGAINST fires: multi-hump vs not. For every group: N, mean dollars per trade as sum/N (hr-062), win rate, and a day-block bootstrap 95 percent CI of the mean (2000 draws, days resampled); for every contrast the difference of means with a day-block CI. Plus descriptive tables: by final letter, and by the context label (AGREES / AGAINST / NEUTRAL / UNKNOWN) x multi-hump. A group with N below 20 is never called a result (hr-008). The cost of a wrong call is read as the gap between the two group means times how often the call is wrong, printed as a rough dollars-per-trade bound, not as an edge.
## 4. Limits
Hindsight shape (K1, K2) is an UPPER BOUND on what knowing the shape could be worth; K3 is the realistic version. The tell fires 10:00-14:30 and a B is readable mainly from about 13:30, so most fires happen before the shape is knowable. Trades on one day are correlated (day-block CIs). Shapes and trades come from the same window that has had many looks; nothing is a finding or a gate, and a contrast that looks large is a hypothesis for a pre-registered test. The CSV is the 2026-10-28 run of `run_trade.py` (V1, split rule); any later change to those rules would not be reflected.
