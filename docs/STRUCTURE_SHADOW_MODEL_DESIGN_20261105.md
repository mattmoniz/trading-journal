# Design: the next-hour RANGE model as a forward SHADOW forecast (written 2026-11-05; NOT BUILT; user 2026-11-05: 'I guess both. What do you think'; for a DeepSeek design critique at off-peak hours BEFORE any live code, hr-031)

Origin: Step A lead (`docs/STRUCTURE_MODEL_STEP_A_PREREG_20261104.md` section 8, claim `structure_model_stepA_20261104`): ln(next-hour range / ADR) is predictable from intraday price-movement state and now-versus-lately readings, +24.9 percent of the HAR-X + clock loss on 1,424 scored explore rows, CI +0.035..+0.055, both halves positive, placebo beaten; the auction-structure and extra-overnight groups added nothing and are NOT used. It is a volatility-state forecast of a RANGE, not a direction and not a trade (two direction-free brackets failed). Shadow only: no orders, no gate, no sizing.
## 1. What it records (one row per trade date and decision time, 10:30, 11:30, 13:00, 14:30 ET)
Features known at the decision minute (exactly step A's M2: the three clock dummies; the HAR-X regressors: ln of the previous session's range / ADR, its 5- and 22-session means, ln of the overnight range through 09:29 / ADR; group D: ln of the range so far / ADR, ln of the IB range / ADR, the IB extensions up and down, the close's position in the day's range, the minutes since the range was last extended, scaled rotation legs per hour via the real `detectRotationLegs`; group C: ln of the last-30-minute range vs its 20-session norm for the same window, ln of the tick-derived 30-minute volume vs its norm, the absolute tick-flow imbalance of the last 30 minutes). Outputs: the predicted ln range, the expected range in points (ADR x exp), and the HAR-X + clock baseline's prediction next to it. The label (the realized next-hour high-low) is attached AFTER the hour ends by a separate step that never touches the prediction.
## 2. Learning schedule (champion / challenger, fixed schedule, no peeking)
CHAMPION = the ridge fitted on all completed explore days and every FORWARD day before today, refit nightly at 20:20 ET and FROZEN as a versioned artifact before the next session (the penalty chosen by time-ordered inner CV); CHALLENGER = the same recipe with a gradient-boosted learner (it lost in explore, +18.2 vs +24.1 percent; kept as a check). A model version is never changed during a session; each row stores its model version and the code hash (the repo's `code_version` convention). The baseline (HAR-X + clock) is refit the same way.
## 3. Forward scoring (pre-registered here, applied by script, not by eye)
Nightly step (after the close): attach labels to the day's rows; counts only until the first look. FIRST LOOK at 60 complete forward days (240 rows), SECOND at 120, then one non-overlapping look per 60: improvement = loss(baseline) - loss(champion) as a percent of the baseline loss, day-block (week-block) bootstrap 95 percent CI, both halves, the same circular-shift placebo as step A. CONFIRMED: CI lower bound above 0 and both halves positive: the forecast is kept as a display. FAILED: at the SECOND look the CI includes 0 or the point estimate is negative: retire to the baseline (HAR-X + clock) and write the autopsy (which decision time and which group lost skill). A decay alarm: a rolling 20-day improvement below 0 for 3 consecutive weeks flags a decision (no automatic action). Detectable improvement is printed with every look.
## 4. Where it lives and what it never does
A table (migration per `docs/DB_MIGRATION_PROTOCOL.md`; regenerate `server/schema.sql`), a nightly script called from `scripts/run_daily_calibration.sh` (a failed night is retried the next night and never fails the job), an on-demand NOW reading (like `scripts/regime/regime_now.py`) for the Monitor card, and ONE section of the single Monitor and Learning page (CLAUDE.md cv-110) with its own written Next line (co-022). It reads bars, ticks and the HAR history only; it never reads a trade or a P&L; nothing reads it for any decision. The server needs a restart only to serve the new Monitor key (cv-112: the page tolerates a missing key). Features come from the SAME code path as the research (hr-022: one shared feature module used by the research script and the nightly job, with a parity test that reproduces step A's rows on explore dates).
## 5. Known limits and open questions for the reviewer
Explore was one era (2023-03 to 2025-05); the later era has no tick data, so the first look is the first true out-of-sample test; tick volume for a live day comes from the Sierra tick files (a feed outage degrades the volume feature: define the fallback (skip the row, never impute)); rotation legs need the JS detector from Python (a subprocess helper); the decision times are fixed (a 14:30 row has the largest gain, +31.7 percent, and the least time to use it); the model forecasts a range, so its only uses are context and stop / target sizing expectations, both of which failed as trades; a hard scope limit: no consumer is wired until a CONFIRMED look and the user's word.
## 6. Questions for the critique
(1) Is the nightly refit-and-freeze schedule free of look-ahead and of 'optional stopping' (cv-006)? (2) Is 60 and 120 forward days the right look cadence given about 4 rows per day and day-clustered rows (detectable effect)? (3) Is the champion / challenger set-up worth its complexity at this N, or should there be one model plus the baseline? (4) What breaks when a tick or bar feed is late or missing at a decision time? (5) Is the parity test between the research features and the live job sufficient (what else could drift)?
