## Part A: pullback limit (simulated dollars, the research V1 simulation)
For each V1 trade (signal bar i, direction d, structural stop, original entry at the open of bar i+1): if bar i+1 closes in the tell direction beyond the signal bar's close (CONFIRMED, as in the one-bar test), place a LIMIT at the signal bar's close c[i] (buy limit for a long, sell limit for a short), live for the next 10 bars (bars i+2..i+11, before the 15:55 flatten, and cancelled if price trades beyond the signal bar's extreme in the tell direction by 1 x the median 20-bar range before filling: no chasing). A limit fills only if price trades THROUGH it by one tick; the fill price is the limit (no improvement); the stop is the ORIGINAL stop; from the fill bar the V1 exit rule applies (`trade.run_position`, with the net adjusted for the fill price: net = net_from_open_entry + d x (entry_open_based - limit price) x $2). A signal whose bar i+1 does not confirm, or whose limit never fills, produces NO trade (net 0, reported as missed). Counted tests (family `tell_pullback_20261102`, ledger tests = 2, BH q = 0.10): TA1 mean net per FILLED trade minus the mean net of all original trades; TA2 the total net per ORIGINAL SIGNAL (missed = 0) minus the original mean (the fair comparison: a filter that skips more than it helps loses). Day-blocked bootstrap CI (3000), halves, at least 150 filled trades on at least 80 dates for a candidate; CANDIDATE = BH survivor, CI excludes zero, filled mean above zero with CI lower bound above zero. Uncounted: fill rate, missed winners (mean original net of the signals the limit missed), by year.
## Part B: market levels choose the side (market behaviour first, on the explore bars; points, not dollars)
Levels at the signal bar (all causal, RTH): VWAP; overnight high and low; initial-balance high and low (after the first hour); prior-session RTH high, low, close and volume POC (from the prior session's 500-contract bars; the first 20 sessions have no baseline and are excluded). At bar i with close c: the nearest level ABOVE (distance du) and BELOW (dl). The rule: if min(du, dl) <= 2 x the median (high - low) of the last 20 bars (price is "near" a level) then direction = SHORT when the nearest level is above (resistance nearer, du < dl) and LONG when it is below (support nearer): fade the nearest level; otherwise no trade. Entry at the open of bar i+1 plus one tick slip; outcome y_H = direction x (close of the last bar completed within H minutes of the entry bar's start - entry price), H = 15, 30, 60, complete windows only. The tell direction is NOT used (the tell is only the timing trigger, as in the user's idea).
Populations: TELL = the 1,760 V1 signal bars; CONTROL = decision-grid bars (every 10th index, 10:00-14:30) with price moving and no tell. Counted tests (family `tell_level_direction_20261102`, ledger tests = 3): H = 15, 30, 60: TELL minus CONTROL in mean y (day-blocked bootstrap CI, 3000) plus the TELL absolute mean; CANDIDATE = BH survivor, CI excludes zero, TELL absolute mean CI lower bound above 1.5 points, same sign in both halves, at least 200 TELL events on at least 100 dates. Because the breakout trade is the exact mirror (y changes sign), a significantly NEGATIVE result means the levels act as launch pads (continuation), and is reported as such (counted in the same test, two-sided). Uncounted: the same rule per level type, tell direction agreeing vs disagreeing with the level-chosen direction (does the tell add anything to the level read), the fade vs breakout split by how near the price is, by year.
Resolution, stated now: about 800-1,000 TELL events resolve a 30-minute mean of about +/-4 points; the control (thousands) resolves about +/-1.5. A cost-sized edge (1.5 points) is visible only if it is in the control as well, which would say the levels, not the tell, carry it.
Reading: no candidate in either part: neither a pullback entry nor level-chosen sides rescue the tell at this resolution. A candidate: a lead for a forward shadow log (the nightly tagger would log distance to each level at every tell), never a live change.
