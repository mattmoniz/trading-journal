# Pre-registration: the stop-run signature at one-second resolution — 2026-10-21

Written and committed BEFORE the per-second price archive is built or any feature or outcome of this test is computed. Origin: the 1-minute version (`STOP_RUN_SIGNATURE_PREREG_20261021.md`, claim `stop_run_signature_bar_level_not_shown_20261021`) showed nothing, but one-minute bars cannot see the sequencing of a stop run. The user (2026-10-21): "test the per second version because it's an aggressive quick move that reverses quickly." Research only; nothing live changes.

## Data (built for this test)
- `scripts/tick_microstructure/extract_persec_px_20261021.py` re-reads the same RTH tick files as the existing per-second aggression archive (`scratch/persec_20261020`, same file-selection and validation: the session's tick volume must match the database RTH volume within 3 percent) and writes, per RTH session, for every second with trades: second of day (ET), buy-aggressor volume, sell-aggressor volume, low, high, last price. Output `scratch/persec_px_20261021/DATE.tsv`. About 836 sessions, 2023-03-07 to 2026-10-19; sessions that fail the volume check are excluded.
- **Integrity check (reported before anything else):** for each session, the lowest and highest tick price inside each full minute must equal the database 1-minute bar's low and high within 0.5 point on at least 95 percent of minutes; a session below that is excluded and counted.
- **Events:** the same replay events as the 1-minute test (already built, not re-tuned): P1 = 862 StopSweep events (`scratch/stop_sweep_replay_events_20261021.json`: V2 mean R and k = 3 R-race expectancy), P2 = 3,084 FAILED_SWEEP_REVERSAL events (`scratch/fsr_replay_events_20261021.json`: dollars at 68/250 and the target-hit share). Events on sessions with no valid per-second file are dropped and the share reported. Explore = to 2025-05-31, hold-out = 2025-06-01 on.

## Features (point-in-time; information through the end of the event minute only)
Event time T = the end (close) of the event 1-minute bar. Window = the 600 seconds before T (the same ten bars as the rule). Extreme second = the second with the lowest low (long) or highest high (short) in the window (first if tied). The 30-second bin containing it (bins aligned to 09:30:00) is the extreme bin.
- **Vs burst of volume:** total volume in the extreme bin, z-scored against that same clock bin over the previous 60 sessions (at least 40 needed).
- **Rs violence of the push:** (high minus low) of the extreme bin in points, z-scored the same way. (Replaces "speed", which needs a unit.)
- **As forced aggression at the extreme:** for a long, (sell-aggressor volume minus buy-aggressor volume) divided by their sum in the extreme bin; mirror for a short.
- **TR snap-back time:** seconds from the extreme second to the first second that trades back through the swept level (long: high at or above the level; short: low at or below it). The level is the event's own level. Events whose level cannot be matched are dropped. Smaller = faster.
- **Bs aggression after:** for a long, (buy minus sell) divided by their sum from the second after the extreme to T; mirror for a short.
No cutoff is chosen by hand: Vs, Rs, As, Bs are tight at or above the explore-period median of that feature, TR is tight at or below the explore-period median; frozen and applied unchanged to hold-out.

## Tests (frozen; identical rules to the 1-minute test)
Five hypotheses H1 Vs, H2 Rs, H3 As, H4 TR, H5 Bs, each in each population: tight minus loose mean outcome, day-blocked bootstrap 95 percent interval of the DIFFERENCE, explore and hold-out separately. KEPT (strong) if the explore interval excludes zero on the favourable side AND the hold-out one does too; KEPT (weak) if explore excludes zero and the hold-out point estimate has the same sign; else NOT SHOWN. Mechanism SUPPORTED only if at least one hypothesis is KEPT in BOTH populations and the hold-out subset tight on every kept hypothesis beats its flipped-direction version (the placebo already stored in each event file) with the interval above zero; subsets under 20 events or 20 distinct days are underpowered. 5 hypotheses x 2 populations x 2 periods = 20 intervals, so about one spurious favourable interval is expected; the replication requirement is the guard. Descriptive, no verdict: the typical timeline (median seconds from extreme to reclaim, volume and aggression profile by second before and after the extreme, for P1 events versus the 1-minute-bar signature features), the 10:00-10:30 share of events that are tight on the kept features.

## Known limits (stated in advance)
The replay events are approximations of the live rules (13 percent and 42 percent fidelity); per-second data still cannot see the order book or which orders were stops; second-level price and aggression come from the tick prints of the front-month contract file while events come from the continuous database bars (checked by the integrity test); the baseline for the burst features uses the same clock bin on prior sessions (the open and the hour boundaries have their own large normal volume); the user's mechanism may depend on features not in prints.
