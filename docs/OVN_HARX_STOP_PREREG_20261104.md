## 1. The trade and the data (unchanged from the overnight order-flow research, `scripts/tell_context/research_20261103/ovn_sim.py`, hr-022: the real `sim()`)
Entry at the 00:00 bar open plus one tick against (`o + d x 0.25`), direction d = the sign of the 18:00-21:00 ask-minus-bid flow (`rows_allnights.csv`, 863 nights, 2023-03 to 2026-11), stop S points, exit at the 14:30 open minus one tick, a stop beyond the market fills at the bar open, a touched stop at its price minus one tick, $2 per point, $2 round trip. Fixed-stop baseline = 400 points (and 300 reported). Nights come from `scratch/ovn_orderflow_20261104/days.json` (minute bars 00:00-15:59, the pre-midnight features).
## 2. The width forecast (causal at 00:00 of the night)
ADR_t = the median RTH range of the 20 sessions before the night. HAR-X features: ln(RTH range of the previous session / ADR_t), its mean over the previous 5 and 22 sessions, and ln((high - low from 18:00 to 23:59) / ADR_t) (the overnight range SO FAR, not the range through 09:30: a stop set at midnight must not use information from after midnight). Target y = ln((high - low of the 00:00-14:30 window) / ADR_t) = the range the trade actually lives through. Expanding walk-forward OLS, refit every 5 nights, warm-up 120 nights after the first 22. Forecast width W_t = ADR_t x exp(prediction).
## 3. Arms (stop = c x width, c set ONCE per arm so that the mean stop over the scored nights equals the fixed stop: a scale normalisation, not tuning; one c per fixed stop, 400 and 300)
HARX (the forecast above); BASEWIDTH (the right control: the baseline model's width = weekday dummies + the previous session's range ratio, the forecast's own baseline); ADRSCALED (the flat ADR, the weaker control); FIXED (400 or 300). Same entry, same exit, same fills: arms differ only in how the stop VARIES with the night.
## 4. Counted tests (3), paired per night, week-block bootstrap CI at 98.33 percent (Bonferroni 3, 4,000 draws, seed 20261104), both chronological halves, detectable effect (2.8 x SE) printed first
T1: mean dollars per trade, HARX minus FIXED-400, all scored nights. T2: HARX minus BASEWIDTH (does the forecast beat plain volatility persistence), all scored nights. T3: HARX minus FIXED-400 on the nights with 6 or more rotation legs by midnight (the regime where the trade has an edge: 87 nights in all, fewer scored; detectable effect printed, expect it to be large). PLACEBO for T1: the width series circularly shifted by a random offset of at least 10 nights (1,000 draws, c re-set to the same mean stop), the real improvement must beat the 97.5th percentile of the zero-centred placebo improvements. LEAD only if the CI lower bound is above 0, both halves are positive and (T1) the placebo gate is beaten.
## 5. Reported, not counted (cv-026: a stop scaled with width changes BOTH the stop-out rate and the loss size, so they are shown SEPARATELY)
Per arm: win rate, mean loss, mean win, stop-out share, worst loss, mean stop, mean dollars by year; the 300-point versions; ADRSCALED vs FIXED; the by-year split of T1 (2023 / 2024 / 2025 / 2026); the pass-race view: the worst night and the 5th percentile night per arm (the trade's risk against the $2k trailing floor).
## 6. Honest limits, stated now
The flow-sign trade has no edge outside the high-rotation regime and mostly in 2026 (`ovn_orderflow` addenda A2-A4), and its 2026 outcomes were ALREADY READ in that research: this is NOT a sealed test and there is no unspent hold-out for this trade; a LEAD is a hypothesis for the forward race (a WIDTH arm in `ovnArms`, forward nights from 2026-11-02), not a result. A stop scaled by a width forecast changes how often the trade is stopped as well as how much it loses; only the paired dollars decide. T3 has few nights. Range, not realized variance, is the volatility proxy. A positive T1 with a negative T2 would mean any width scaling (persistence) helps, not our forecast.

## 9. A1. Corrections after DeepSeek round Y (`scratch/deepseek_response_20261104_192523.md`, 2026-11-05 ~00:40 UTC)
Round Y found the features causal at 00:00 (the overnight range is 18:00-23:59 only: `n_pre = 360`), the target, the walk-forward and the `sim()` call identical to the research trade. Three corrections: (1) the scale constant `c = S / mean(width over the scored nights)` uses FUTURE nights' widths (hr-020, technically): it is one scalar applied to every night, so it cannot reorder or change which arm is better on a night, it shifts the stops by a few percent and the -1,970 / -802 tail headline by tens of points; a causal expanding-mean c would fix it and was not run. (2) The placebo used 300 draws, not the 1,000 written in section 4; its 97.5th percentile is noisier. (3) The 'fatter tail' is a DESIGN consequence of holding the MEAN stop at 400 (the worst night's stop was about 984 points), not a finding about the forecast; a capped arm (stop = min(c x width, 400)) bounds the tail by construction (never wider than the fixed stop, so the worst loss cannot exceed the fixed stop's) but its mean dollars are unknown and untested. The conclusion (no detectable gain over the fixed stop or the baseline-width control) is unaffected.
